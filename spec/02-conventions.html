02 · Conventions

Scientific conventions & voice

The rules that make the brand scientifically trustworthy. They are encoded as components in the library so they cannot be skipped. Every gene name italicized, every σ factor superscripted, every evidence code labelled and badged.

Do

Wrap every biological name in its component, even inside paragraphs. Screen-readers benefit from the semantic class, future i18n filters depend on it.

Don't

Italicize a generic <span style="font-style: italic">araC</span>. Without the class the meaning is lost.

Conventions · Sequence

Sequences & coordinates

Sequences are uppercase and grouped in 10‑nucleotide blocks, optionally with a leading position number. Coordinates are right‑aligned monospace with comma thousand separators.

Conventions · Voice

Voice & writing

Third-person scientific. Descriptive, not promotional. Sentences are factual statements, not invitations.

Casing

  • Object names use exact biological casing — never auto-titled. Genes lowercase italic; proteins title-case roman; operons lowercase italic.
  • UI labels (nav, buttons, table headers, tabs) use sentence case. Acronyms preserve uppercase: TF, ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, GO, FAIR, IDA.
  • Section eyebrows / micro-labels are uppercase with 0.05–0.06em letter-spacing, in --text-tertiary.

What we never do

  • No emoji in product copy. Unicode mathematical glyphs (σ, Δ, −, ±) are content, not decoration — not the same as emoji.
  • No exclamation marks. No "you" in headings. No first-person plural marketing copy ("our database", "we have curated…").
  • No abbreviations unless they’re scientific (TF, TFBS, HT, GO, ChIP-seq).
